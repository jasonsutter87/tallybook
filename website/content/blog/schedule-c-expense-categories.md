If you're a sole proprietor or a single-member LLC, your business profit lands
on **Schedule C** of your Form 1040. Part II lists the expense lines, and the
most common question at tax time is simply: *where does this go?*

Here's every expense line, with examples. The rule underneath all of them: an
expense is deductible when it's **ordinary and necessary** for your business,
meaning common in your line of work and helpful for it.

## The expense lines

| Line | Category | What usually goes here |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | Advertising | Ads, sponsored posts, a booth at a fair, business cards, your website's hosting if it's mainly marketing |
| 9 | Car and truck expenses | Business use of your vehicle: the standard mileage rate or actual costs, with a mileage log |
| 10 | Commissions and fees | Marketplace and platform fees, payment-processing fees, sales commissions |
| 11 | Contract labor | Freelancers and contractors you paid (some need a 1099-NEC) |
| 13 | Depreciation and section 179 | Larger equipment, like a computer or camera, written off over time or at once (Form 4562) |
| 14 | Employee benefit programs | Benefits for employees, not for yourself |
| 15 | Insurance (other than health) | Liability, professional, property and business insurance |
| 16a | Mortgage interest | Interest paid to a bank on a mortgage for business property |
| 16b | Other interest | Interest on business loans and business credit cards |
| 17 | Legal and professional services | Lawyers, accountants, bookkeepers, tax preparation for the business |
| 18 | Office expense | Software subscriptions, postage, small office items |
| 19 | Pension and profit-sharing plans | Plans for employees (your own retirement contributions go elsewhere on Form 1040) |
| 20a | Rent: vehicles, machinery, equipment | Leased equipment or vehicles |
| 20b | Rent: other business property | Office, studio or workshop rent |
| 21 | Repairs and maintenance | Fixing equipment or business property, not improving it |
| 22 | Supplies | Materials and supplies used up in the work |
| 23 | Taxes and licenses | Business licenses, permits, payroll taxes you pay as an employer (not your income tax) |
| 24a | Travel | Airfare, lodging and transport while traveling away from home for business |
| 24b | Deductible meals | Business meals: generally only **50%** is deductible |
| 25 | Utilities | Power, internet and phone used for the business |
| 26 | Wages | Pay to employees, never to yourself |
| 27a | Other expenses | Anything else ordinary and necessary; you list each item in Part V |

Line 30, separately, is the **home-office deduction**. The simplified method
allows **$5 per square foot of dedicated office space, up to 300 square feet**,
so at most $1,500, and it can't create a loss.

## The mistakes that trip people up

### Paying yourself

What you take out of a sole proprietorship is an **owner's draw**, not wages and
not an expense. It doesn't go on Schedule C at all. Your profit is taxed whether
you leave it in the business account or move it to your personal one.

### Estimated tax payments

Your quarterly federal estimated payments are personal income tax, so they're
**not** a business expense (and not line 23). Record them as owner's draws. (See
[how quarterly estimates work](/blog/quarterly-estimated-taxes-safe-harbor/).)

### Meals at 100%

Book business meals at what you paid, but only half goes on line 24b. If your
books show $1,200 of meals, Schedule C shows $600. Keeping the full amount in
your books and applying the 50% at tax time avoids confusion later.

### Software: office expense or depreciation?

Monthly or yearly software subscriptions are usually **office expense**. A large
one-time equipment purchase, like a computer, is usually **depreciation** (often
taken all at once under section 179 or the de minimis safe harbor). This is the
line most worth a quick question to a pro.

### Personal and mixed-use costs

Your phone or internet bill used half for work? Deduct the business share and
keep a note of how you worked it out. Purely personal spending, even when paid
from the business account, isn't deductible; it's another owner's draw.
